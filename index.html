DOTWORLD

A fun personal experiment, built in a conversation with Claude. Nothing here is my achievement alone.

This is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to OpenStreetMap, the OpenStreetMap Foundation, OpenFreeMap, OpenMapTiles, MapLibre, the World Bank, the Wikimedia Foundation, ColorBrewer or Nothing. No claim is made over any of their work.

This project exists only because those projects gave their work away. It points at them — it does not compete with them, replace them, or take anything from them. The map data, the tiles and most of the code underneath belong to the people below. If you like what you see, the credit is theirs.

Map data

© OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open Database Licence (ODbL) 1.0 — every road, coastline and building here is the work of volunteer mappers.

Tiles & rendering

  • OpenFreeMap — free planet-wide vector tiles, no API key, created and paid for by Zsolt Ero
  • © OpenMapTiles — the vector tile schema those tiles are built on; its credit must stay visible
  • MapLibre GL JS (BSD-3-Clause) — does all the hard work: tiles, projection, globe, WebGL. The dot lattice is a thin pass over their output
  • Nominatim (OpenStreetMap Foundation) — place search, country lookup and framing

Data

  • World Bank Open Data (CC BY 4.0) — world population, birth and death rates, urban share, land area, and the population of whichever country you pick
  • GeoNames (CC BY 4.0) — the population of every city in the world over 15,000 people, and the time zone of each capital
  • Wikidata (CC0) — the 195 capitals with their populations and country codes, the best-known monuments of every capital, Montpellier's monuments
  • Natural Earth (public domain) — the country outline that lights up when you pick one
  • Monument ranking is by Wikipedia language coverage — really the work of Wikipedia's editors

Colour & type

  • ColorBrewer — Cynthia Brewer & Mark Harrower, Penn State. The population ramp is their YlOrRd, reversed so brightness rises with population on black
  • Dotwork, Space Mono, Space Grotesk — SIL Open Font License 1.1
  • The dot-matrix look is inspired by Nothing's visual language. Their Ndot typeface is licensed for Nothing brand materials only and is deliberately not used or shipped here
